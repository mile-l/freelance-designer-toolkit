How to use these calculators

Start with the quote builder before you send a price. Break the job into phases and estimate the hours for each — discovery, design, revisions, handoff — then add a buffer of 20% for the things you cannot see yet. A buffer on the hours (or on the price) is what keeps a fixed-price project profitable when the brief turns out to be vaguer than it looked.

Cap revisions in writing. Two revision rounds is the industry-standard allowance for most design work. Every round after that is extra work at your hourly rate — the revision calculator shows exactly what that is worth, and the clause it generates puts the rule in your contract so the conversation never gets awkward.

After the project, run the scope creep calculator. Compare the hours you quoted with the hours you actually worked. If your effective hourly rate came out far below your nominal rate, the next quote needs a bigger buffer, a tighter scope, or a higher price. Most freelancers discover they are donating 20–30% of their time this way.

Frequently asked questions

How many revision rounds should I include in a design project?

Two rounds is the common standard for logo, brand, and web design work: one round for direction, one for refinement. Include the number in your contract, define what counts as a round (for example, one consolidated list of changes), and price further rounds at your hourly rate.

How do I charge for extra revisions?

Multiply the hours one round takes by your hourly rate, and state the per-round price in the contract before work starts. When the client asks for a round beyond the allowance, send a short change order with the price and get written approval before you do the work.

What is a good buffer for a fixed-price freelance quote?

20% is a reasonable default for work you have done before; use 30% or more for a new type of project, a vague brief, or a client you have not worked with. The buffer covers estimation error and small scope changes — it is not profit, and it should not be negotiated away.

How much deposit should I ask for?

50% before work starts is standard for projects under a few thousand dollars, with the balance on delivery or at agreed milestones. A deposit does two jobs: it confirms the client is serious, and it caps what you lose if the project stalls.
